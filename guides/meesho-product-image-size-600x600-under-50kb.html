Meesho Product Image: 600×600 px Under 2MB

Square catalog photos, JPEG, and a file-size target you can actually defend — plus why 50KB is not a Meesho rule. This URL still contains “50kb” from an older rumor; the requirement we document is under 2MB.

Meesho sellers usually start from phone photos taken on the shop floor. Those originals are often 3–8 MB — slow to upload on mobile data and easy for a catalog form to reject. The workflow that actually matches the tools on this site: crop to square → resize to 600×600 → compress until the file is under 2MB, then confirm the live cap in your Supplier Panel.

What Meesho actually publishes

Meesho does not publish a single public table that says “exactly 600×600 and under 50KB.” Treat third-party blogs that invent those numbers as unverified.

  • Meesho Supplier — How it works says that when you upload a catalog you pick a category, add images, and that detailed image guidelines are provided in the Meesho Supplier Panel. That panel is the source of truth for pixel and file-size caps on the day you list.
  • Meesho — Sell clothes online asks for a clear front image in JPEG, with no logo or watermark, no partial product crops, and no props.

On this site we still use 600×600 as the working square size because Meesho catalog cards are square and that preset is built into our Image Resizer. If the panel asks for a larger square (for example 1000×1000), use that number instead — the same steps apply.

Why we dropped the 50KB story

Older seller posts (and an earlier version of this page) treated “under 50KB” as a Meesho rule. It is not. Forcing a 600×600 product photo down to 50KB usually means JPEG quality far below 70%, which smears fabric, jewelry edges, and label text. Buyers on cheap phones still see a blurry thumbnail.

File-size story we use now: stay under 2MB so the image is small enough for mobile uploads, then stop. If the Supplier Panel rejects the file, lower quality one step at a time. Do not chase 50KB.

Step 1 — Resize to 600×600

  1. Open Image Resizer.
  2. Upload the product photo.
  3. Click the Meesho 600×600 preset, or type 600 × 600. Crop to square first if the shot is landscape.
  4. Download the resized file.

Step 2 — Compress under 2MB

  1. Open Image Compressor.
  2. Upload the square file.
  3. Choose JPEG. Leave quality around 85% unless the file is still over 2MB.
  4. Download, then upload to your Meesho catalog. If the panel shows a different max size that day, follow the panel.

Common mistakes

  • Uploading full-resolution phone photos without resizing to a square.
  • Using PNG for simple product-on-white shots (larger files, no benefit for Meesho JPEG catalogs).
  • Crushing the file to 50KB so texture disappears — that was never a Meesho requirement.
  • Watermarks, logos, or props on the main image — Meesho’s clothing help page disallows these.

Frequently asked questions

What size should Meesho product images be?

Meesho catalog cards are square. A practical working size is 600×600 px. Confirm the live minimum in the Meesho Supplier Panel — Meesho’s public how-it-works page says detailed image guidelines appear there.

Is 50KB a Meesho requirement?

No. That figure is a seller-blog rumor, not a published Meesho rule. Forcing 50KB often destroys fabric texture. Keep files under 2MB so mobile uploads succeed, and only compress further if the panel rejects the file.

JPG or PNG for Meesho?

Meesho’s clothing seller page asks for JPEG without logos or watermarks. Use JPG for product-on-white shots. PNG is larger and rarely needed unless you need transparency.

My photo looks blurry after compressing — what went wrong?

You may have started from a soft image or compressed far below 70% quality. Shoot in good light, resize to a square first, then compress until the file is under 2MB — not until it hits 50KB.

Can I batch process many SKUs?

This site processes one image at a time in the browser. Repeat the same resize-then-compress steps for each SKU and keep master originals on disk.
