How this page is meant to be used

Our tools compress, resize, and convert photos in the browser. They cannot fix a dark phone photo or a shaky handheld shot. This page lists the physical kit Indian marketplace sellers usually add before they open Image Compressor or Image Resizer.

These are not reviews of named ASINs. Amazon inventory in India turns over constantly. Linking a single SKU that vanishes next week would be dishonest. Each category has a budget search and a premium search so you land on current listings in that class. Read recent buyer photos and return rates on Amazon before you pay.

Typical seller order of operations: light the product → lock the phone on a tripod → shoot on a plain backdrop → resize/compress here → print labels and pack. Skip a step and no image tool will fully rescue the listing.

Ring lights for product photography

A ring light is the cheapest way to flatten shadows on jewelry, cosmetics, and small apparel. Budget lights under ₹500 work on a desk for 15–20 minutes at a time; they often run hot and the color temperature can shift. Premium 10-inch dimmable rings with a stand let you match daylight-ish white (around 5500K) so Meesho and Amazon thumbnails do not look yellow on one SKU and blue on the next.

After you shoot, use our compressor so the file stays under the marketplace upload cap without another round of phone-gallery “save as.” Lighting first, compression second.

Typical range: ₹400–1500

Phone tripods for sellers

Handshake blur is the usual reason a 600×600 Meesho crop looks soft even after sharpening. A tabletop tripod plus a 2-second timer (or a cheap Bluetooth shutter) keeps the camera plane consistent across a 50-SKU session so every main image has the same height and angle.

Budget mini-tripods flex. If you shoot garments on a hanger, a taller aluminum stand with a phone clamp is worth the extra rupees. Do not expect a ₹300 plastic stick to hold a large Android at 1.5 metres.

Lighting kits for product shoots

Ring lights fail on large apparel and boxes because the light is small and close. Two softboxes (or LED panels with diffusion) left and right of the product, slightly above camera height, is the standard catalog setup. Budget kits are often underpowered; premium LED kits dim without a color shift.

Amazon India main images want a clean white field. Even lighting is what gets you there — not a “white background” filter after a dim phone shot.

White backdrop for product photos

A wrinkled bedsheet photographs as grey. Paper rolls or a collapsible white panel give a continuous sweep so you do not have a horizon line through the product. Meesho clothing help asks for a clear product shot without props; Amazon main images want white (RGB 255,255,255) behind the item.

If the backdrop still looks dirty after the shot, our tools will not paint it white. Fix lighting and paper first, then crop and compress.

Shipping label printers for Meesho sellers

Thermal 4-inch printers print AWB labels without ink. On a 40-order day that is hours saved versus a home inkjet that jams on sticky labels. Budget USB models need a PC; Bluetooth models talk to the Meesho/Amazon Android apps. Check label width (usually 4×6 inches) against the courier template before you buy.

This has nothing to do with image tools except workflow: sellers who batch-print labels usually also batch-prep photos the night before. Keep masters on disk; upload compressed JPGs only.

Typical range: ₹2500–6000

Digital weighing scales for packages

Courier bills use charged weight. A ₹400 scale that drifts by 50g is expensive over a month of COD. Look for 1g or 5g resolution up to 30kg for apparel and small goods; 50kg platforms if you ship kitchenware. Tare (zero with the carton on the plate) is not optional.

Typical range: ₹400–1200

Packaging tape and bubble wrap

Returns from crushed cartons cost more than a roll of wrap. Thin “gift wrap” bubble pops in one transit. For glass and ceramics, use small-bubble wrap plus a rigid box; for fabric, a polybag plus tape is usually enough. A tape dispenser that does not chew the roll pays for itself in a week of packing.

Typical range: ₹200–600

Courier bags for Meesho / Amazon shipping

Poly mailers should match the SKU, not a one-size-fits-all 16×20 that the courier bills as volumetric weight. Tamper-evident bags reduce “empty packet” disputes. Keep a few sizes: 6×8 for jewelry, 10×12 for tees, 14×16 for kurtas. Do not ship liquids in unlined poly without a secondary seal.

Typical range: ₹150–500

Barcode scanners for inventory

Once you pass a few dozen SKUs, typing codes into a sheet is how you ship the wrong size. A USB HID scanner that types into Excel or the seller app is enough for a tabletop packing bench. Wireless scanners help if the printer and laptop are on opposite walls. Confirm it reads the barcode type on your labels (usually Code 128).

Typical range: ₹800–2500

Storage racks for inventory

Photo sessions go faster when SKUs are already binned by size/color instead of dumped in a carton. Four-layer plastic racks work for apparel; steel racks if you store jars or hardware. Label bins with the same SKU you use in Meesho so the photo folder on your phone matches the shelf.

Typical range: ₹1000–3500
